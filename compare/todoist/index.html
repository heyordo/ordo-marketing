Ordo vs Todoist

Both can turn a messy input into separate items. Ordo keeps the result in Now, Next, and Someday; Todoist turns it into tasks within a fuller planning system.

Updated October 4, 2026

Ordo: Brain Dump is a free iPhone app for capturing mixed thoughts by typing or speaking. It separates a dump into individual items and sorts them into Now, Next, and Someday. No account is required.

Choose Ordo when

You want one place for tasks and non-task ideas, with a simple Now, Next, Someday result and no account.

Choose Todoist when

You want a brain dump to become structured tasks inside a system with projects, recurring dates, collaboration, and cross-platform access.

The main difference

Todoist's Ramble turns spoken thoughts into organized tasks, and its Task Capture can extract tasks from pasted text or files. Ordo accepts a typed or spoken mixture and sorts the resulting cards by when they matter: Now, Next, or Someday. Todoist goes deeper on task management; Ordo stays deliberately smaller.

Where Todoist is better

Todoist is the stronger choice for ongoing project management, recurring work, collaboration, integrations, and people who need the same system on several devices. Ramble also gives it a real voice brain-dump option.

Where Ordo is better

Ordo is a smaller system for people who want to capture tasks, reminders, and ideas together without turning every thought into a managed task. Its Now, Next, and Someday view is the organizing decision; there are no projects or labels to maintain, and no account is required.

Can they work together?

Yes. Use Ordo to capture and clarify a mixed dump, then move complex or collaborative work into Todoist. Or keep Ordo as the whole system if Now, Next, and Someday is enough.
