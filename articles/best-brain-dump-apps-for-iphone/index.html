Choosing a capture tool

Best brain dump apps for iPhone

Six real options. Where each is better, and what each asks you to trade off.

By Ordo · October 5, 2026

The best brain dump app depends on what you want to happen after you get a thought out of your head. A blank note, a task manager, an email inbox, and an automatic sorter solve different problems. Here are six iPhone options, with a reason to choose each and a reason you might not.

Editorial disclosure: Ordo wrote this guide and is one of the apps below. This compares documented features; we are not claiming hands-on testing of the other apps. Details were checked against their makers' documentation and App Store listings on October 5, 2026. Features and availability can change.

Ordo: for sorting a mixed dump

Ordo: Brain Dump is a free iPhone app for capturing mixed thoughts by typing or speaking. It separates a dump into individual items and sorts them into Now, Next, and Someday. No account is required.

Its strength is the moment when a task, reminder, and loose idea arrive together: you can capture them in one go, then review and correct the separate cards. If you are offline, Ordo saves the whole dump right away as one card with a quick on-device guess; it splits and sorts the cards properly when you reconnect.

The trade-off is important: Ordo's full sorting sends dump text through its Cloudflare service to Anthropic. Saved items remain on your iPhone, but this is not an entirely on-device sorting tool. Read the privacy policy and FAQ before putting sensitive thoughts into any app.

Choose Ordo if you want a small, no-account capture-and-sort workflow more than a full project manager.

Apple Notes: for a flexible page you can keep

Apple Notes lets you write a quick thought or keep a detailed note with checklists, images, sketches, scanned documents, and collaboration. It is the stronger choice when the note itself is worth preserving or sharing.

It can hold a brain dump perfectly well. The difference is what happens next: if you want separate Now, Next, and Someday items, you make those decisions yourself. That may be a feature, not a flaw, if you want a blank page and complete control.

Choose Apple Notes if you want long-form notes, attachments, or shared reference material more than automatic sorting.

Drafts: for capture first, routing later

Drafts opens ready for text and supports dictation. Its actions can send or transform a draft into another app or service. That makes it especially useful if you already have a system and want a fast front door to it.

Drafts is better than Ordo for customized text workflows and sending captured material to several destinations. The trade-off is that you may need to choose or configure actions yourself. Its core approach is to capture text and decide what to do with it, not automatically produce Ordo's time-based cards.

Choose Drafts if you want a flexible text inbox and are willing to shape your own workflow.

Todoist: for turning a dump into managed tasks

Todoist's Ramble can turn spoken thoughts into structured tasks, including details such as dates, priorities, and projects. Todoist also offers recurring tasks, projects, and collaboration. It is a serious alternative for voice brain dumps, not merely a place to type one task at a time.

Todoist is better when captured thoughts need to become part of a lasting task system, especially shared or recurring work. Ordo stays smaller and keeps loose non-task ideas alongside tasks. Ramble is currently available on all plans; other feature access can vary by plan.

Choose Todoist if your brain dump is mostly actionable tasks and you want the system that manages them afterward.

Things 3: for intentional planning

Things 3 has an Inbox for unprocessed thoughts and lists for Today, Upcoming, Anytime, and Someday. It also supports projects, repeating tasks, reminders, and calendar views. It is a stronger choice than Ordo for planning work deliberately over time.

Things does not ask you to organize before capturing, but it expects you to return to the Inbox and make those planning decisions. That manual step can be valuable if you prefer to decide exactly where each task belongs. Things is a paid app, with separate purchases for its different Apple platforms.

Choose Things 3 if you want a polished task-planning system and do not mind processing your inbox yourself.

Braintoss: for sending a thought to your existing inbox

Braintoss captures typed thoughts, voice notes, and photos, then delivers them to an email or webhook destination. That is a different kind of brain dump: the app gets the thought into a place you already check.

Braintoss is better if email is your trusted inbox or if photographing a whiteboard or receipt is part of your capture habit. Ordo is better if you want the result separated and sorted inside the capture app. With Braintoss, review and organization happen at the destination you choose.

Choose Braintoss if sending something to your existing inbox is more useful than keeping another list.

Which one should you start with?

Try the tool that matches your next step, not the one with the longest feature list. For a blank page, start with Apple Notes. For a customizable text inbox, Drafts. For an email handoff, Braintoss. For ongoing task management, Todoist or Things. For one mixed capture that becomes a short Now, Next, and Someday list, try Ordo.

Whichever you choose, test it with a real mixed dump: one task, one reminder, one idea, and one thing you are not ready to classify. The useful app is the one that makes both capture and review easy for you.
