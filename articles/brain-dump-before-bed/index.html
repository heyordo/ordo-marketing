Brain dump before bed: a five-minute handoff for tomorrow

Capture what came to mind tonight. Decide what to do with it at a short daytime review.

By Samuel Laredo, Ordo founder · November 1, 2026

You put your phone down and remember the library book. Then the email you meant to send. Then an idea for the weekend. None of these needs a full plan right now, but they do need somewhere to go.

A brain dump before bed is a short capture, not a challenge to organize tomorrow perfectly. Write down what showed up, mark anything with a real time, and leave the bigger decisions for a daytime review.

If you are new to the method, start with our five-minute brain dump guide. The evening version below keeps the capture brief and leaves most decisions for tomorrow.

1. Choose one place to capture

Use a notepad, a blank note on your phone, or an app you already know. Keep the bar low: one place, five minutes, no folders or tags to set up. If you prefer to put your phone away at night, paper is a good choice.

Write fragments exactly as they come. “Return library book,” “ask Jordan about invoice,” and “maybe try a new pasta recipe” are complete enough. You can separate tasks from ideas later.

2. Do not turn the dump into a late-night project

The capture might look like this:

“Return library book. Email Jordan about invoice. Buy oat milk. Try that pasta recipe. Call the dentist Thursday.”

You do not need to open five apps and schedule every line. Read the dump once. Is there one thing that genuinely needs attention tomorrow morning? Mark it. If “call the dentist Thursday” is an appointment you have already made, put the actual time in your calendar. If it is only a task you hope to do Thursday, it can stay on a task or reminder list.

Leave the recipe idea alone. Not every thought needs a deadline.

3. Give the rest a review time

Write “review tomorrow” at the top of the page, or set one reminder for a realistic time. The point is to know when you will look again. Tomorrow, you can move items into a calendar, a reminder list, or a place for ideas—and discard anything that no longer matters.

If you regularly find your nighttime list growing, make the next-day review smaller, not the nighttime sorting more elaborate. Start with the one item that actually has a time or consequence.
