Choosing a capture tool

Brain dump app for iPhone: what to look for

The best brain dump app should make capture easier, not give you another system to maintain.

By Samuel Laredo · October 4, 2026

A brain dump often contains several kinds of thoughts at once: a task, an idea, a reminder, an errand, and something you are not ready to classify. The right app should let that mixture arrive before asking you to organize it.

1. Fast mixed capture

Look for a single input where you can type or speak several thoughts without stopping to choose a project, folder, priority, or date. If setup comes before capture, the app may feel slower than a blank note.

2. A useful review step

Capturing thoughts is only half the job. The app should make it easy to review what you entered, separate distinct items, correct wording, move an item, or delete something that did not need to become a task.

3. Categories you can understand

More categories are not always better. A simple system such as Now, Next, and Someday can be enough when the question is when something deserves attention. A project manager may be better when you need dependencies, labels, recurring tasks, and shared projects.

4. Reminders without forcing dates onto everything

A useful brain dump app should preserve ideas that do not have a date while still letting genuinely timed items become reminders. Not every thought needs a deadline.

5. Honest offline behavior

“Works offline” can mean different things. Check whether you can capture without a connection, whether sorting happens on the device, and whether a saved dump waits to be processed later. An app should state the difference clearly.

6. Clear privacy language

Find out where saved items live, whether text leaves the phone for processing, how long service providers may retain it, whether an account is required, and what analytics or crash reports are collected. A short slogan is not a substitute for a detailed privacy policy.

How Ordo approaches it

Ordo: Brain Dump is a free iPhone app for capturing mixed thoughts by typing or speaking. It separates a dump into individual items and sorts them into Now, Next, and Someday. No account is required.

Your saved items live on your iPhone. Sorting requires a connection: the dump text passes through Ordo's Cloudflare service to Anthropic, and Ordo does not store it in a content database. Anthropic's standard API retention can be up to 30 days. You can move or edit every sorted item.

When a different tool is better

Choose Apple Notes for long-form writing, attachments, scans, or shared notes. Choose a full task manager such as Todoist for projects, recurring tasks, collaboration, integrations, or cross-platform planning. Choose paper for complete offline use, no data transmission, free-form drawing, and a screen-free process.

Ordo is for the narrower moment before those systems: getting mixed thoughts out quickly and deciding what deserves attention now, next, or someday.
